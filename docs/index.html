How a prediction is made

  1. The photo is resized to 256 × 256 and normalized with ImageNet mean and std, the same way as in training.
  2. The U-Net returns one logit per pixel. A sigmoid turns it into the probability that the pixel belongs to the pet.
  3. The probability map is resized back to the photo's size first, then the threshold is applied, so mask edges stay smooth.

This page runs the INT8 model with ONNX Runtime Web. Its output is identical to ONNX Runtime in Python for the same input.

Freezing the pretrained encoder worked better and trained faster on a CPU. On the held-out test set (3,662 images) the final model reaches IoU 0.900 and Dice 0.946.

Where it fails

  • Small pets: IoU drops to 0.85 when the pet covers less than 20% of the photo.
  • Distractors such as toys and teddy bears get segmented as pets.
  • Dark pets on dark backgrounds, unusual poses and occlusion.
  • Thin parts like ears, legs and tails, and fur edges.

The model always looks for one cat or dog. A photo without a pet can still produce a mask.
